Bin marker strips — colour

Print at 100% scale — no "fit to page", or the bars stop being whole units. Use matte stock: a gloss finish under a ceiling lamp throws a specular highlight that washes the colour out of exactly the bars being measured. Cut on the dashed line. The white at the two ends is part of the marker — it is what tells the scan where the strip stops; the few millimetres above and below are only for cutting.

Bin:

  • Mount inside, where a shut bin hides the strip. Visible means open, and that is the entire signal.
  • Mount level. The scanner walks rows and columns, so a strip lying flat or standing upright is found and one at an angle is not.
  • Yellow, magenta and cyan are the three process inks, each laid down by a single cartridge. That is why they reproduce consistently between printers — and why they sit further apart in the camera's colour space than any palette mixed from them.
  • Bind each strip to a bin in Settings → Marker Strips, then open a bin with the debug overlay showing and tap Calibrate colours once. That records what this room's light actually does to the print.
  • Watch the two numbers in the debug overlay while placing them. unit px is samples per printed unit — below six the bar widths stop separating and the strip is named by its ink alone; raise --unit in this file. The ×n after it is how many scan lines cross the strip — three is the floor; raise --bar-height.
  • The strip is deliberately long and thin, because that is the shape of the rim it goes on. Its length and its height are unrelated, so both can be set to whatever the bin allows.
